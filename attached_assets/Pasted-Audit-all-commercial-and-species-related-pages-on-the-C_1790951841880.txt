Audit all commercial and species-related pages on the Crownwing Parrots website and fix the problem of repetitive, generic wording.

The current issue:
Several commercial pages use very similar copy, repeat the same selling points, and do not provide enough species-specific information. This makes the pages less useful to visitors and creates weak or duplicate-style SEO content.

Your task is to rewrite and expand these pages so that every important commercial page has unique, useful, natural, species-relevant content.

### Requirements

1. Audit all existing commercial pages
Identify pages such as:
- Parrots for sale
- African parrots
- Macaws
- Cockatoos
- Amazons
- Conures
- Caiques
- Eclectus
- Parakeets
- Small psittacines
- Individual species/breed pages
- UK location/city commercial pages
- Availability/enquiry pages

Do not remove working functionality, routes, metadata, images, structured data, forms, or internal links.

### 2. Remove generic repeated copy

Do not simply change a few words between pages.

Avoid repeating generic paragraphs such as:
- "Our parrots are raised with care."
- "We provide healthy and beautiful birds."
- "Contact us to learn more."
- "We are passionate about parrots."

Each page must have a clear purpose and information relevant to the species or commercial intent of that page.

### 3. Make species category pages genuinely useful

For every major parrot category, include relevant information such as:

- General temperament
- Typical personality
- Approximate lifespan
- Typical adult size
- Noise level
- Social needs
- Intelligence and training
- Talking ability where relevant
- Diet
- Housing and cage requirements
- Exercise requirements
- Enrichment needs
- Suitability for first-time parrot owners
- Suitability for families
- Common behavioural challenges
- Commitment required
- Questions buyers should consider before enquiring

Do not invent veterinary claims, guarantees, certifications, legal claims, or availability.

### 4. Differentiate each category

The content should clearly communicate the differences between species groups.

For example:

African parrots:
Focus on intelligence, communication, mental stimulation, sensitivity, routine and long-term commitment.

Macaws:
Focus on large size, powerful beaks, space requirements, enrichment, high social needs, noise and experienced ownership.

Cockatoos:
Focus on extremely social behaviour, attention requirements, noise, emotional needs and destructive behaviour if under-stimulated.

Amazons:
Focus on confidence, vocal ability, strong personalities, social interaction and seasonal behaviour.

Conures:
Focus on playful behaviour, compact size, social nature, noise differences between species and active households.

Caiques:
Focus on high energy, physical play, curiosity, strong personalities and enrichment.

Eclectus:
Focus on temperament, sexual dimorphism, nutrition considerations, routine and social interaction.

Parakeets and small psittacines:
Focus on smaller-space ownership, flock behaviour, enrichment, companionship and differences between smaller species.

### 5. Improve individual species pages

Every individual bird/species page should have meaningful sections such as:

- Overview
- Appearance
- Personality and temperament
- Size
- Expected lifespan
- Talking and vocalisation
- Intelligence
- Training
- Diet and nutrition
- Housing
- Exercise
- Social requirements
- Enrichment
- Who this species may suit
- Important ownership considerations
- Availability / enquiry CTA
- Related species

Keep the provision for up to four species photos already planned for these pages.

Do not number the birds.

### 6. Improve commercial intent without keyword stuffing

The website should naturally target relevant UK searches such as:

- parrots for sale UK
- parrots for sale
- African grey parrots UK
- macaws UK
- cockatoos UK
- Amazon parrots UK
- conures UK
- caiques UK
- Eclectus parrots UK
- parakeets UK
- parrot breeder UK
- hand-reared parrots UK
- parrots available UK

Use keywords naturally.

Do NOT create spammy paragraphs filled with locations or repeated keywords.

### 7. Improve UK city pages

City pages must not simply swap the city name in identical text.

Each city page should contain useful regional context while remaining truthful.

Use sections such as:

- Parrots for owners in [City]
- Species enquiries
- Preparing for parrot ownership
- Collection / transport information, only where supported by the existing business model
- Questions before enquiring
- Popular species information
- Links to relevant species categories
- Links to ownership guides
- Enquiry CTA

Avoid fake statements such as claiming Crownwing Parrots has a physical branch, breeder facility, showroom or local staff in a city unless this is actually confirmed in the project data.

### 8. Strengthen internal linking

Add contextual internal links between:

Species category → individual species

Individual species → parent category

Commercial pages → relevant species

Species pages → care/ownership guides

Guides → relevant commercial/species pages

UK city pages → major commercial pages and relevant species categories

Use descriptive anchors instead of repeatedly using "click here."

### 9. Improve page structure

Use clean semantic headings:

One H1 per page.

Then logical H2 and H3 sections.

Break long text into useful sections.

Avoid giant walls of text.

Keep the current Crownwing visual style and responsive layout.

### 10. Add useful calls to action

Calls to action should match where the visitor is in the buying journey.

Examples:

"Explore African Parrot Species"

"View Macaw Species"

"Learn About Parrot Ownership"

"Ask About Current Availability"

"Speak to Crownwing Parrots"

"Find the Right Parrot for Your Home"

Avoid aggressive sales wording.

### 11. Protect SEO

Preserve existing working URLs wherever possible.

Review and improve:
- title tags
- meta descriptions
- H1 headings
- internal links
- canonical tags
- Open Graph metadata
- structured data

Do not create duplicate titles or duplicate meta descriptions.

Do not generate doorway pages or thin SEO pages.

### 12. Content quality standard

Every page should answer:

"What information does this page provide that the other parrot pages do not?"

If the answer is "almost nothing," rewrite it.

Prioritise helpful content for real prospective parrot owners over filling pages with keywords.

### 13. Final audit

After implementation:

- Run the project build.
- Fix TypeScript/build errors.
- Fix broken internal links.
- Check mobile responsiveness.
- Check that every major commercial page has substantially unique content.
- Check that category pages link to their relevant species.
- Check that individual species pages link back to categories.
- Check for obvious duplicate paragraphs across pages.
- Check title/meta duplication.
- Do not leave console errors or warnings introduced by this work.

At the end, give me a concise report showing:

1. Pages rewritten
2. Pages expanded
3. New sections added
4. Internal links added or improved
5. SEO metadata improved
6. Any remaining pages that still need original content

Do not stop after auditing. Implement the improvements directly in the Crownwing Parrots project.
