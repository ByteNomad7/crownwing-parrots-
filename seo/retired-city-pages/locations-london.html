Parrots for London homes

London spans the capital’s boroughs and the wider Greater London area, so a useful parrot plan starts with the particular home rather than an assumed city lifestyle. Think through your rooms, household routine and the bird’s needs before narrowing a shortlist.

Regional planning guide

This page helps owners prepare an enquiry. It is not evidence of local Crownwing premises, local stock or a confirmed collection or delivery service.

Map the space you can genuinely offer

Measure the enclosure location, access for cleaning and the floor area available for supervised activity. In a home with shared walls or compact rooms, consider sound, ventilation and how the bird can move without obstructing everyday household use.

A smaller footprint does not make every small species suitable, and a large room alone does not meet a bird’s social needs. Compare adult size, activity and equipment requirements for the exact species before buying an enclosure.

Compare species before choosing a favourite

Parakeets and other small psittacines include different species with distinct social and vocal patterns. Conures also vary; their compact size says little by itself about noise or the interaction they need. African Grey Parrots call for their own careful consideration of mental activity and routine.

Read the relevant species profiles, then ask which individual is being discussed, its known age, established diet and observed behaviour. A category description or photograph cannot answer questions about an individual bird’s preferences.

Make shared living arrangements explicit

If you rent, share walls or live with housemates, check the practical permissions and discuss how calls may carry at different times of day. Do not rely on a broad claim that a species is quiet; ask about normal vocal behaviour and plan for natural calls.

Agree who will feed, clean, interact and supervise exercise. Include quiet places where the bird can retreat from activity, and consider whether household members can respect those boundaries consistently.

Questions to settle before an enquiry

Prepare questions about the bird’s identity, history, current food, handling preferences and any records available. Ask what has been directly observed and distinguish that from information that is not known. Request recent images of the individual if appropriate.

Describe your available space, daily schedule, experience and any existing animals. Ask what further information is needed to assess a possible match, and do not assume an enquiry establishes availability or an agreed arrangement.

Confirm any proposed travel arrangements

Do not make travel plans on the basis of a London location page. If an individual enquiry progresses, establish directly whether any viewing or handover can be arranged and what transport plan, if any, is actually proposed.

Before agreeing to a journey, confirm the details, responsibilities and suitable carrier for that bird with the relevant people. Keep the bird’s welfare and a calm, prepared transition at the centre of the discussion.

Prepare for the long view

List the recurring work your household can maintain: fresh food and water, cleaning, enrichment, supervised time and a plan for days away. Identify suitable veterinary support independently and consider how care would continue if your schedule or address changed.

Use the downloadable enquiry to organise your questions rather than treating it as a message sent to the business. The buying checklist and species information can help you decide what remains uncertain before making a commitment.
